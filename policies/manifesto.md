**Created: October 16, 2026**

Social networks should be about the people in our lives. Modern social media has neglected that in favor of monetizing our data and attention.

That's why I'm creating Musta with one primary goal:

> Help us stay connected without sacrificing our privacy.

I am building it with these three pillars:
- Your data is private by default
- Real connections, not an audience
- Encourage intentional actions

All design and product decisions will be evaluated against these pillars. If a choice contradicts any of them, it does not belong in Musta.

## Your Data is Private by Default

Musta is an app for you to document your life and selectively share those moments with the people who matter. Your entries are private by default and you control who sees them. For your future self only? That's the default. You want to share an entry with your significant other, your family, or your close friends? That's also possible.

That privacy extends to all the information you provide: your name, your email, the people you're connected with, even your presence in the app. Your data is private by default and will only be shared with other people on Musta you've chosen.

Your data will never be sold or shared for advertising purposes.

However, this privacy promise is not universal. Exceptions include, but are not limited to:
- Disclosure in response to a legal request
- The app using and processing your information in order to work

For more information, refer to the [Musta privacy policy](/privacy-policy).

## Real Connections, Not an Audience

I'm designing Musta to help you connect with people you personally know. Not necessarily close to, but people you've met in real life or online: high school or college friends, current or former colleagues, extended relatives, close friends, immediate family, that person you met in a bookshop, online gaming buddies, and that person you met in an app. Those are the connections I want the app to help maintain and grow.

Here in Musta, there is no audience to perform for, no followers to keep happy, and no likes to count. If you choose to share moments from your life, they are treated as precious memories shared with people who care about you, not content to be consumed by an audience.

## Encourage Intentional Actions

With Musta, I want to answer a question social media has abandoned:

> "Musta na sila?" *("How are they?")*

The only updates you'll see are about the people you're connected to. Your feed will end when you've seen the latest update from everyone in your circles. Missing the endless content of social media? That is a feature, not a bug. Most days are ordinary and quiet, and that is okay. Musta will not manipulate you into endlessly scrolling or pressure you into constantly creating "content".

Other social networking features of Musta are designed around deliberate actions such as:
- Visiting a person's profile is the primary way to see every update they've shared with you. Your feed will only show each person once with their latest update.
- Image uploads are limited to encourage selection and curation. Personally, I find it more enjoyable to browse through an old physical album with a dozen different photographs than a Facebook album with hundreds of near-identical images.

These are just some of the features and choices in Musta that you will not see in conventional social media apps. Friction causes lower engagement and, in existing apps, that is an important metric to optimize for. Friction slows us down and in Musta, those speed bumps are deliberately placed to help people slow down, think, and become more intentional when interacting or sharing with others.

And if someone hasn't updated in a while? Visiting their profile will give you the option to send them a gentle "Musta ka na?" *("How are you?")* nudge to let them know you're thinking of them. Sometimes all someone needs is to know they are remembered.

## How Musta Will Sustain Itself

A deliberate choice in support of the privacy pillar is how Musta sustains itself: a minimal monthly subscription. The project needs to fund itself without selling your data or showing ads, and a subscription is the cleanest choice. The monthly subscription's primary use is to keep the project online and financially sustainable, which means covering infrastructure, development, and maintenance costs. The subscription price will be as low as possible to make Musta accessible to everyone but still enough for the project to sustain itself. The current target is about ₱30 per month (or ₱300 per year) with the final amount announced 30 days before public launch. A pricing transparency page will also be created to show the project's cost and revenue.

During early access, Musta will be free for anyone invited.

An always-free tier is also being planned. As most of the forecasted cost revolves around media storage and bandwidth, trade-offs will be done around those things to keep the core features intact. More details will be shared as the project is developed.

If the project can't sustain itself or has to be shut down for any other reason, everyone will be notified 30 days in advance to allow them to download and export their data.
